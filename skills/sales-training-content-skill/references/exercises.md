# Training Exercises & Activities Library

This library contains proven training exercises for B2B sales and CS training. Each exercise includes purpose, setup, facilitation notes, and debrief guidance.

**Design philosophy: play before pedagogy.** People retain more and defend less when they're laughing than when they're being lectured. Every exercise here should generate energy in the room, not just cover content. Favour competition, silliness, sound effects, physical movement, and quick wins over long explanations. Debriefs should be short and land the point fast, not turn the fun back into a lecture. If an exercise isn't generating at least a few laughs or a bit of friendly competitive noise, cut the setup time and raise the stakes (a bell, a scoreboard, a daft prize, a time pressure) rather than adding more instructions.

## Listening & Awareness Exercises

### Active Listening Quiz (Logical Listening)

**Purpose:** Demonstrate that people jump to conclusions and don't truly listen, a critical weakness in sales conversations.

**Duration:** 15-20 minutes

**Setup:**
1. Tell participants you'll read 14 questions quickly
2. They cannot ask you to repeat
3. They write answers numbered 1-14

**Questions:**
1. "An electric train is travelling south and the wind is blowing from the east. Which way will the smoke blow?" → *No smoke, electric train*
2. "You've dug a hole six feet deep and six feet wide. How much dirt is in the hole?" → *None, you removed it*
3. "Some months have 30 days, others 31. How many have 28?" → *All 12*
4. "If a plane crashes on the border of US and Canada, where are survivors buried?" → *Survivors aren't buried*
5. "Before Mount Everest was discovered, what was the world's highest mountain?" → *Still Everest*
6. "Mr. and Mrs. Jones have five daughters, each with one brother. How many in the family?" → *8 (parents + 5 daughters + 1 son)*
7. "Which is correct: 'the yolk of the egg is white' or 'the yolk of an egg are white'?" → *Neither, yolk is yellow*
8. "A one-storey house is completely green inside. What colour are the stairs?" → *No stairs*
9. "Five Frenchmen speaking different languages need to sit so neighbours can communicate. How?" → *They all speak French*
10. "A farmer has 20 pigs and all but 17 die. How many left?" → *17*
11. "A rooster on a roof lays an egg. Which way does it roll?" → *Roosters don't lay eggs*
12. "Most is greatest, brag is boast, ghost is spirit. What do you put in a toaster?" → *Bread (not toast)*
13. "According to the Bible, how many animals did Moses take on the ark?" → *None, it was Noah*
14. "You're racing and overtake the person in second place. What position are you in?" → *Second*

**Debrief Questions:**
- "Why did you get these wrong?"
- "What assumptions did you make?"
- "When does this happen on sales calls?"

**Link to Training:** We hear "not interested" or "we have a solution" and assume we know what they mean. We stop listening and start defending.

### Losing the Fillers

**Purpose:** Build awareness of verbal fillers (um, like, basically, you know) that undermine credibility and confidence.

**Duration:** 30-40 minutes

**Setup:**
1. Pre-record or source two call recordings: one with many fillers, one clean
2. Prepare random images (objects, scenes, abstract art)
3. Get a bell, horn, or buzzer

**Exercise Flow:**
1. Play both recordings, ask "Which salesperson would you buy from?"
2. Distribute one image per participant (can repeat if large group)
3. Each person has 60 seconds to describe their image
4. Everyone else rings bell/makes noise when they hear a filler
5. Track filler count for each person

**Facilitation Notes:**
- First few people will be nervous, this is good, it shows the pressure
- Encourage use of pauses instead of fillers
- "Silence is more powerful than 'um'"

**Debrief Questions:**
- "What caused you to use fillers?"
- "How did the bell affect your speaking?"
- "What's the alternative to filling silence?"

**Link to Training:** Fillers happen when brain catches up to mouth. In sales, controlled delivery = confidence = credibility.

## Questioning Skills Exercises

### Kipling's Six Servants

**Purpose:** Foundation for effective questioning in discovery and objection handling.

**Duration:** 20-25 minutes

**Setup:** Share Kipling's poem:
> "I keep six honest serving-men / They taught me all I knew / Their names are What and Why and When / And How and Where and Who"

**Exercise:**
1. Give participants a scenario (e.g., "Prospect says they're not hitting revenue targets")
2. They must write one question for each type:
   - **What:** "What specifically is causing the shortfall?"
   - **Why:** "Why do you think this is happening now?"
   - **When:** "When did you first notice this?"
   - **How:** "How is this affecting the team?"
   - **Where:** "Where in the pipeline is the breakdown?"
   - **Who:** "Who else is impacted by this?"
3. Share and discuss which questions open vs. close conversation

**Debrief Questions:**
- "Which question type do you default to?"
- "Which type do you rarely use?"
- "Which questions got the best responses in discussion?"

**Link to Training:** Most salespeople ask too many "what" and not enough "why" or "how", the questions that reveal motivation.

---

### The Rephrase Challenge

**Purpose:** Train the skill of rephrasing objections to uncover the real underlying issue.

**Duration:** 20-30 minutes

**Setup:** Prepare 5-8 common objections

**Exercise:**
1. Trainer reads a surface objection
2. Participants write a rephrase in 60 seconds
3. Share answers, discuss best versions

**Example Objections and Rephrases:**

| Surface Objection | Poor Rephrase | Good Rephrase |
|-------------------|---------------|---------------|
| "Why do I need to pay for this?" | "So you're asking about cost?" | "What would be the impact if you chose not to have this capability?" |
| "Send me an email" | "So you want more info?" | "It sounds like this isn't a priority right now, what would need to change for it to become one?" |
| "We're happy with our current provider" | "So you don't want to switch?" | "What made you choose them, and what would make you consider an alternative?" |
| "I need to think about it" | "What do you need to think about?" | "What's the main thing you're weighing up?" |

**Debrief Questions:**
- "What's the difference between repeating and rephrasing?"
- "Why does rephrasing work psychologically?"

**Link to Training:** Rephrasing moves the buyer from System 1 (defending) to System 2 (thinking). It shows you understood them AND advances the conversation.

### The Isolation Drill

**Purpose:** Practice isolating objections before attempting to answer them.

**Duration:** 25-30 minutes

**Setup:** Pairs, scenario cards with objections

**Exercise:**
1. Partner A raises objection
2. Partner B must isolate BEFORE answering
3. Mandatory phrase: "And is this the only thing stopping you from moving forward?"
4. If Partner A says "No, there's also...", go back to listening
5. If Partner A says "Yes", then proceed to answer
6. Switch roles after 2 scenarios each

**Facilitation Notes:**
- Watch for people who skip isolation
- Praise genuine isolation attempts even if awkward
- Time pressure helps (2 minutes per scenario)

**Debrief Questions:**
- "How many times did isolation reveal additional concerns?"
- "What happens if you answer an objection without isolating first?"
- "Why do salespeople skip this step?"

**Link to Training:** Most salespeople answer the first objection without checking for others. This wastes time and leaves hidden concerns that kill deals later.

## Context-Setting Exercises

### Businesses That Changed

**Purpose:** Set context for why companies must adapt, and why prospects need what you sell.

**Duration:** 15-20 minutes

**Setup:** Prepare slides with company logos (Nokia, Netflix, Amazon, IBM, etc.)

**Exercise:**
1. Show each logo
2. Quiz: "What did this company originally do?"
   - Nokia → Paper mill, then rubber boots, then phones
   - Netflix → DVD by mail
   - Amazon → Online bookstore
   - IBM → Tabulating machines
   - Nintendo → Playing cards
3. Discussion: "Why did they change? What happens to companies that don't?"

**Debrief Questions:**
- "What forced these changes?"
- "How does this apply to our prospects?"
- "What's the cost of not adapting?"

**Link to Training:** Sets up the "challenge" or "insight" portion of your methodology. Prospects also need to adapt or face consequences.

---

### The Status Quo Audit

**Purpose:** Make the cost of inaction tangible.

**Duration:** 20 minutes

**Exercise:**
1. Pick a real prospect scenario (or use case study)
2. Calculate the cost of the current problem:
   - Per week
   - Per month
   - Per year
   - Per person affected
3. Calculate the opportunity cost:
   - Revenue not captured
   - Time not saved
   - Risk not mitigated

**Discussion:**
- "Why don't prospects do this math themselves?"
- "How can you help them see this?"

**Link to Training:** Loss aversion is powerful. Quantifying status quo pain is more motivating than benefit selling.

## Role-Play Structures

### Structured Objection Handling Role-Play

**Duration:** 40-60 minutes (depending on group size)

**Setup:**
1. Pairs or triads (third person observes)
2. Scenario cards with specific objections
3. Framework reference cards

**Structure:**
1. Salesperson opens call/meeting
2. At 2-minute mark, prospect raises objection
3. Salesperson applies framework (e.g., LEARN)
4. Debrief after each round (2 minutes)
5. Rotate roles

**Observer Checklist:**
- [ ] Did they pause before responding?
- [ ] Did they acknowledge/empathise?
- [ ] Did they ask a diagnostic question?
- [ ] Did they isolate before answering?
- [ ] Did they reframe to the real issue?
- [ ] Did they close with a next step?

**Scenarios to Use:**
1. Cold call, "We're not interested"
2. Discovery, "We already have a solution"
3. Proposal, "This is too expensive"
4. Close, "I need to run this by my boss"
5. Follow-up, "Now isn't a good time"

### Live Call Fishbowl

**Purpose:** Real-time learning from actual calls.

**Duration:** 30-45 minutes

**Setup:**
1. One participant makes a real call (or uses realistic simulation)
2. Rest of group observes on mute
3. Whiteboard for live note-taking

**During Call:**
- Group notes what went well
- Group notes missed opportunities
- No interrupting

**After Call:**
- Salesperson self-assesses first
- Group shares observations (positive first)
- Trainer synthesises key learnings

**Important:** This requires psychological safety. Frame as learning, not judgment.

## Cognitive Bias Exercises

### The Bias Spotter

**Purpose:** Train recognition of buyer biases in real scenarios.

**Duration:** 25-30 minutes

**Setup:** Prepare 6-8 buyer statements

**Exercise:**
1. Read buyer statement
2. Participants identify which bias is operating
3. Discuss how to respond

**Example Statements:**

| Buyer Says | Bias | Response Strategy |
|------------|------|-------------------|
| "We've always done it this way" | Status-Quo | Challenge with insight about market changes |
| "Last time we tried this, it was a disaster" | Restraint/Bad Experience | "What would have needed to be different?" |
| "Everyone uses [competitor]" | Familiarity | Introduce differentiated value |
| "What if it doesn't work?" | Loss Aversion | Mitigate risk, offer proof/pilot |
| "I've already committed to [alternative]" | Post-Purchase Rationalisation | Respect decision, plant seeds for future |

---

### System 1 vs System 2 Demonstration

**Purpose:** Make the two thinking systems tangible.

**Duration:** 10-15 minutes

**Exercise:**
1. Ask group: "What's 2 + 2?" (instant answer, System 1)
2. Ask: "What's 47 x 23?" (pause, effort, System 2)
3. Ask: "Are you interested in..." (instant "no", System 1)
4. Discuss: "How do we move someone from automatic 'no' to considered thinking?"

**Link to Training:** Every technique we teach is designed to shift buyers from System 1 (defensive, automatic) to System 2 (analytical, decision-making).

Pairs alternate asking questions with mandatory 3-second pause before responding.

Given a feature, flip it to a benefit in under 10 seconds.

## Playful Practice for the Newer Frameworks

These exercises are built for maximum energy first, framework practice second. Keep instructions to under a minute; let the game teach the lesson.

### Mapping Mayhem (Stakeholder Mapping Framework)

**Purpose:** Practice spotting the four buying roles fast, under pressure, without overthinking.

**Duration:** 15 minutes

**Setup:** Small teams, a stack of "character cards" with silly stakeholder personas (e.g. "Colin, who says yes to everything and approves nothing", "Priya, who has read every security whitepaper twice"). Stopwatch and a bell.

**Exercise:**
1. Deal each team a jumbled set of 6 to 8 character cards for one fictional deal
2. On "go", teams race to sort the cards into Economic Buyer, User Buyer, Technical Buyer, and Coach, then slap the bell when done
3. First team to bell in reads their mapping aloud; other teams heckle or cheer any disagreements
4. Award a genuinely pointless prize (a Post-it note "trophy" works) to the fastest correct team

**Debrief:** "Which role is easiest to miss in a real deal? Why?"

**Link to training:** Speed under pressure mirrors how fast reps actually have to size up a room in a live meeting.

---

### Mind Blown (Insight-Led Reframe Script)

**Purpose:** Practice landing a reframe with real conviction, and make instant feedback fun rather than awkward.

**Duration:** 20 minutes

**Setup:** Everyone gets a scenario card with a customer situation. A "Mind Blown" sound effect on someone's phone, or just a dramatic gasp on cue.

**Exercise:**
1. One at a time, each person delivers just the Warmer and Reframe steps (30 seconds max) to the group
2. The group votes by gesture: hands on head and "mind blown" noise if it landed, a polite golf clap if it didn't
3. Anyone who gets a genuine "mind blown" from at least half the room wins the round
4. Run two or three rounds so people can steal the best lines they heard from others

**Debrief:** "What made the ones that landed actually land?"

**Link to training:** Removes the fear of sounding contrived by turning the reframe into a game with a scoreboard, not a performance review.

---

### Fence Sitters (Indecision Diagnostic)

**Purpose:** Build fast, instinctive recognition of stalling language, separate from genuine objections.

**Duration:** 20 minutes

**Setup:** One volunteer plays an exaggerated, comically indecisive buyer ("I just want to run it past my dog first"). Everyone else has a bell or a buzzer sound.

**Exercise:**
1. The "buyer" stalls with increasingly absurd lines
2. Reps buzz in with one step of the Indecision Diagnostic (assess, recommend, contain, remove the risk) as fast as they can
3. The group votes thumbs up or down on whether the response actually reduced risk or just added more persuasion
4. Rotate the "buyer" role every few minutes so everyone gets a turn being ridiculous

**Debrief:** "Which step of the diagnostic do we skip most often in real life, and why?"

**Link to training:** The absurdity of the stalling lines takes the pressure off, so people experiment with directness they'd normally hold back on with a real prospect.

### Bounce It Back (Reversing Technique)

**Purpose:** Drill the reversing technique until it's reflexive rather than something people have to think about mid-call.

**Duration:** 10 to 15 minutes

**Setup:** Pairs, standing if space allows. This is meant to feel like a fast game of verbal tennis.

**Exercise:**
1. Partner A fires a blunt question ("What does it cost?", "Why should I switch?", "Can you just send a proposal?")
2. Partner B must reverse it into a question within 3 seconds, no pausing to think
3. Any hesitation over 3 seconds and Partner A wins the point, gets loud about it
4. First to 5 points switches roles

**Debrief:** "Which reverses felt natural? Which felt clunky, and what would smooth them out?"

**Link to training:** Time pressure and mock scorekeeping force the instinctive response the technique is actually meant to build.

---

### Red, Amber, Green Freeze (Customer Health Scoring)

**Purpose:** Build a shared, fast, intuitive sense of what healthy vs at-risk signals actually look like, as a physical game rather than a spreadsheet exercise.

**Duration:** 15 minutes

**Setup:** Clear a bit of floor space. Prepare a stack of short account snapshot cards (usage trend, sentiment, last QBR outcome, support tickets).

**Exercise:**
1. Trainer reads an account snapshot aloud
2. Everyone physically moves to a marked spot in the room: Red, Amber, or Green, as fast as they can
3. Anyone who freezes in the wrong colour once the trainer reveals the "correct" call has to do a silly forfeit (star jump, one-line impression of a panicking CSM, whatever fits the room)
4. Run 8 to 10 rounds, increasing snapshot ambiguity as it goes

**Debrief:** "Which snapshots split the room? What signal were we disagreeing about?"

**Link to training:** Physical movement and mild public forfeit make the abstract idea of a weighted health score concrete and memorable.

---

### Win the Room (Business Review Structure)

**Purpose:** Contrast a poor business review with a strong one, played for laughs before being taken seriously.

**Duration:** 20 to 25 minutes

**Setup:** Two volunteers per round, one plays CSM, one plays customer.

**Exercise:**
1. Round one: CSM delivers a deliberately terrible QBR, all usage dashboards, no outcomes, robotic delivery. Ham it up
2. Round two: same CSM, same customer, now runs the four-part Business Review Structure (recap goals, show outcomes, surface risks, realign on plan) properly
3. Audience scores both rounds out of 10 for "would I renew after this"
4. Swap pairs and repeat with a new scenario

**Debrief:** "What was the single biggest difference in how the good version felt?"

**Link to training:** Watching the bad version played for comedy makes the failure pattern unforgettable, and makes the good version land by contrast.

## Customer Success & Account Growth Exercises

These are for the CS crew — CSMs, Onboarding Specialists, Renewals Managers, and Account Managers. Same rule as everywhere else: get them laughing and competing first, land the framework second.

### Re-Qualify or Bust (SPICED Through the Lifecycle)

**Purpose:** Drill the idea that discovery never ends — you re-qualify at every lifecycle stage, especially at renewal, using SPICED (Situation, Pain, Impact, Critical event, Decision).

**Duration:** 20 minutes

**Setup:** Small teams. A stack of "lifecycle cards" for one fictional account, each stamped with a stage (Kickoff, Day 30, Mid-term, Pre-renewal) and a few facts that have quietly changed since sign-up (new VP, budget freeze lifted, a merger, the champion got promoted). A bell per team.

**Exercise:**
1. Deal each team the same account, one lifecycle stage each
2. On "go", teams have 3 minutes to write one sharp SPICED question for their stage that surfaces what has changed
3. Slap the bell when done; teams read their question aloud in stage order so the account's story unfolds like a soap opera
4. Room votes for the single question most likely to catch a renewal risk nobody saw coming — winning team gets the daft prize

**Facilitation Notes:**
- Push back hard on any team that just re-reads the original Situation — the point is that the situation moved
- Pull real texture from live data where you can: a `get_call` transcript excerpt makes a great "here's what they actually said at kickoff" card

**Debrief Questions:**
- "Which stage do we go on autopilot and stop asking?"
- "What changed in this account that a renewal-only check-in would have missed?"

**Link to Training:** Renewals are lost in the eleven months of not re-qualifying, not in the renewal call. SPICED is a lifecycle habit, not a first-meeting form.

### QBR Card Scramble (Business Review Ordering Race)

**Purpose:** Burn in the value → roadmap → risks → expansion running order of a strong business review, at speed. (Different from "Win the Room", which contrasts good vs bad delivery — this one is a pure ordering race.)

**Duration:** 15 minutes

**Setup:** Each team gets an identical shuffled deck of chunky cards: some are the four backbone beats (Value delivered, Roadmap, Risks & blockers, Expansion opportunities) and some are decoys ("show every dashboard tab", "apologise for a support ticket", "wing it"). Clear a table per team. A bell.

**Exercise:**
1. On "go", teams race to lay the four backbone cards in the correct order and bin the decoys
2. First team to bell in with the right sequence and a clean discard pile wins the round
3. Loser teams must explain, loudly, why one decoy is so tempting in real QBRs
4. Run three rounds, swapping in trickier decoys ("lead with the upsell") each time

**Facilitation Notes:**
- The tempting trap is opening with expansion before proving value — call it out with glee when a team does it
- Keep rounds fast; the whole joy is the scramble

**Debrief Questions:**
- "Why does value have to come before the ask?"
- "Which decoy nearly fooled your team, and why is it so seductive?"

**Link to Training:** A business review that leads with the upsell reads as a sales pitch; value first earns the right to talk expansion.

### The Champion Went Quiet (Churn-Signal Spotting)

**Purpose:** Sharpen the nose for qualitative early-warning signals — the soft, human ones a numeric health score misses. (Different from "Red, Amber, Green Freeze", which sorts snapshots into a RAG status — this one hunts subtle narrative tells.)

**Duration:** 15-20 minutes

**Setup:** Read out short "week in the life of an account" vignettes, each hiding one quiet churn tell (the champion stops replying, meetings get delegated down, "we're reviewing all vendors this quarter", logins shift to one lone user, they suddenly want everything in writing). Everyone has a party horn or a klaxon noise.

**Exercise:**
1. Trainer narrates a vignette in a deadpan, everything's-fine voice
2. The instant someone hears the warning signal, they blast the horn and name it
3. First correct call scores a point; a false alarm on a red herring loses one, so people can't just spam the horn
4. Escalate the subtlety each round until the tells are barely there

**Facilitation Notes:**
- The comedy is the mismatch between your calm narration and the alarm going off
- Great source material: a real `get_call` excerpt where a champion's tone cools off between calls

**Debrief Questions:**
- "Which signal is easiest to explain away in the moment?"
- "What would you do in the next 24 hours after spotting it?"

**Link to Training:** Numbers lag; humans leak. The champion going quiet is often the earliest and loudest signal on the account, if you're listening for it.

### Trade Never Concede (Renewal Negotiation Rounds)

**Purpose:** Make "never give a discount without a trade" a reflex under renewal pressure.

**Duration:** 15 minutes

**Setup:** Pairs, standing if you can. One is the price-squeezing customer, one is the Renewals Manager. A visible scoreboard. Fast rounds, buzzer-timed.

**Exercise:**
1. Customer opens with a discount demand ("knock 20% off or we walk")
2. Renewals Manager has 10 seconds to respond — every response must attach a trade (longer term, case study, reference call, earlier commitment, wider rollout)
3. Any free concession, or any "let me see what I can do" with nothing asked back, loses a point instantly and gets a klaxon
4. First to 5 points switches roles; loudest, most outrageous trade of the session wins a bonus point

**Facilitation Notes:**
- Watch for the sneaky freebie disguised as goodwill — "I'll just round it down for you" is a lost point
- Reward creative trades, not just discipline; the funniest legitimate trade should win something

**Debrief Questions:**
- "Which trades felt fair to the customer and which felt like flannel?"
- "What made you want to just cave? How did you catch yourself?"

**Link to Training:** A concession given for free teaches the customer to squeeze again next year. Every give must buy you something.

### Past the Feature Request (Voice-of-Customer Job-to-Be-Done)

**Purpose:** Train reps to dig underneath a stated feature request to the real job the customer is trying to get done.

**Duration:** 20 minutes

**Setup:** One volunteer plays a customer clutching a very specific, very literal feature demand ("I need a big red export button, top right, exactly there"). Everyone else is on the dig.

**Exercise:**
1. The "customer" states their oddly specific feature request and defends it stubbornly
2. Reps take turns asking one question each to peel back toward the underlying job ("what do you do right after you export?", "who's waiting on that file?")
3. The customer only reveals the real job (e.g. "my boss wants a Monday morning summary and I'm rebuilding it by hand") once someone asks a genuinely good job-to-be-done question
4. Whoever cracks it wins; the room then invents three better solutions than the big red button

**Facilitation Notes:**
- Coach the volunteer to cling to the literal request until a real "job" question earns the reveal
- Feed the personas from live signal where you can — `most_requested_feature` and `top_objections` are gold for realistic, current requests

**Debrief Questions:**
- "How many questions did it take to get past the button to the job?"
- "How often do we just build or promise the button?"

**Link to Training:** Customers ask for features; they hire us for jobs. Shipping the literal request without the job is how you build the wrong thing and still lose them.

## Support & Complaints Exercises

For Customer Support Agents, Technical Support Specialists, and Complaints Handlers. High energy, absurd personas, and a hard focus on the order you do things in — because in support, sequence is everything.

### LAER Relay (Buzz-In Step Drill)

**Purpose:** Drill the LAER sequence (Listen, Acknowledge, Explore, Respond) so Acknowledge never gets skipped on the way to a fix.

**Duration:** 15-20 minutes

**Setup:** Trainer plays a parade of absurd complaint personas ("my invoice gave me a papercut", "your app made my cat leave me"). Teams have buzzers or a nominated noise.

**Exercise:**
1. Trainer voices a complaint, then freezes
2. Teams buzz in with the correct next LAER step and a line that fits it
3. The catch: if anyone jumps to Respond (a fix) before Acknowledge has happened, they lose a point and get a comedy "wrong answer" buzzer
4. Play through full complaints, step by step, so a team can only "win" a complaint by walking all four steps in order

**Facilitation Notes:**
- The absurd personas keep it loose; the sequence rule keeps it rigorous
- Pull genuine opening lines from `top_objections` or a `get_call` excerpt to mix real complaints in with the silly ones

**Debrief Questions:**
- "Why is Acknowledge the step we skip when we're confident we know the fix?"
- "How did it feel as the 'customer' when someone jumped straight to Respond?"

**Link to Training:** A correct answer delivered before the customer feels heard still feels like being dismissed. LAER is an order, not a menu.

---

### Keep Your Cool (De-escalation Under Fire)

**Purpose:** Practise lowering the temperature — naming the emotion, controlling pace, staying level — while a customer gets progressively, comically more irate.

**Duration:** 20 minutes

**Setup:** One volunteer plays an escalating customer who ratchets up the outrage every 20 seconds. The rep faces them. Everyone else watches the rep's tone like hawks.

**Exercise:**
1. The customer starts annoyed and climbs toward theatrical fury
2. The rep must bring the temperature down: name the emotion ("I can hear how frustrating this is"), slow the pace, lower the volume, take ownership
3. If the rep matches the customer's rising tone — voice up, pace up, defensiveness — the room calls "forfeit" and a fresh rep taps in
4. Whoever keeps the customer talking calmly the longest wins; award bonus points for the best emotion-naming line

**Facilitation Notes:**
- Coach the customer-actor to reward genuine calming with visibly dropping their own energy — it should feel like the rep has a dial
- The forfeit tap-in keeps everyone alert and stops one person hogging the hot seat

**Debrief Questions:**
- "What specifically dropped the temperature? What cranked it up?"
- "How did controlling your own pace change theirs?"

**Link to Training:** You cannot out-argue an angry customer, only out-calm them. Tone is contagious in both directions — you choose which way it spreads.

### Five-Step Scramble & Spot the Forbidden Move (Complaints Handling)

**Purpose:** Lock in the 5-step complaints sequence (Acknowledge, Empathise, Investigate, Resolve, Follow-up) and build instant recognition of the moves that torch trust.

**Duration:** 20 minutes

**Setup:** Two quick games back to back. For the scramble: teams get a shuffled deck of the five step cards plus a couple of decoys. For spot-the-forbidden-move: a stack of "handler response" cards, some clean, some containing a cardinal sin. A bell.

**Exercise:**
1. **Scramble:** teams race to lay the five steps in order and bin decoys; first to bell in correctly wins the round
2. **Spot the forbidden move:** trainer reads a handler's response aloud; the instant it contains a forbidden move — blaming the customer, a hollow "sorry you feel that way", or promising beyond authority ("I'll refund everything, guaranteed") — players buzz and name the sin
3. Correct catch scores; buzzing a clean response loses a point
4. Fastest sinner-spotter over the set wins the daft prize

**Facilitation Notes:**
- Read the forbidden-move cards totally straight — the comedy is in how smooth a bad line can sound
- Best source of realistic forbidden moves is live data: `qa_critical_errors` gives you actual cardinal sins your team has committed to dramatise

**Debrief Questions:**
- "Which forbidden move is easiest to say by accident under pressure?"
- "Why does Follow-up get dropped, and what does dropping it cost?"

**Link to Training:** The five steps only work in order and only if you avoid the trust-killers. A great resolution wrapped in a hollow apology still fails.

### Root Cause or Buzzer (First-Contact-Resolution Diagnosis)

**Purpose:** Train the funnel from symptom to root cause before proposing a fix, so first-contact resolution is real and not a guess.

**Duration:** 20 minutes

**Setup:** A volunteer plays a customer reporting a vague symptom ("it's just broken", "the thing won't thing"). Everyone else diagnoses. A loud buzzer.

**Exercise:**
1. Customer states a fuzzy symptom
2. Reps take turns asking one diagnostic question each, funnelling from broad to specific toward the actual root cause
3. Anyone who blurts a solution before the root cause is nailed gets buzzed and sits the next question out
4. Whoever pins the true root cause with the fewest premature solutions wins; the customer then confirms whether the eventual fix would actually have worked

**Facilitation Notes:**
- Coach the customer to keep the symptom genuinely ambiguous so early solutions miss
- The buzzer on premature solutions is the whole lesson — make it satisfyingly loud

**Debrief Questions:**
- "How many questions did it take to reach the real cause?"
- "What made us want to jump to a fix so early?"

**Link to Training:** A fix aimed at the symptom bounces back as a repeat ticket. Diagnose the root cause once and you resolve on first contact.

### The Apology That Lands (Quick Energiser)

**Purpose:** Turn a hollow, generic apology into a specific, owned, action-paired one — fast.

**Duration:** 5-10 minutes

**Setup:** Put a limp apology on the screen: "We're sorry for any inconvenience caused." A 20-second timer. Everyone in.

**Exercise:**
1. Reveal the hollow apology; groan at it together
2. Each person has 20 seconds to rewrite it into something specific ("what"), owned ("we", not "any"), and paired with an action ("here's what I'm doing now")
3. Best three go head to head; room votes with a cheer-o-meter
4. Winning line goes up on the wall as the house standard

**Debrief Questions:**
- "What separated the ones that landed from the ones that still felt hollow?"
- "Which is hardest under pressure — being specific, owning it, or committing to an action?"

**Link to Training:** "Sorry for any inconvenience" apologises for nothing and to no one. A real apology names the problem, takes ownership, and points to the next action.

## Manager & Coaching Exercises

For Sales Managers and anyone doing the coaching. These mirror how the platform actually scores and calibrates — evidence-backed, criteria-first, on a 1-4 competency scale.

### The Calibration Game (Score the Same Call)

**Purpose:** Expose scoring spread and build a shared, calibrated sense of the 1-4 competency scale.

**Duration:** 20-25 minutes

**Setup:** Everyone gets the same short call excerpt and the same competency's criteria. Scoring cards numbered 1 to 4, held face down. A drumroll optional but encouraged.

**Exercise:**
1. Everyone privately scores the excerpt on the chosen competency, 1 to 4, using only the criteria
2. On the count of three, everyone flips their card at once — reveal the full spread to the room
3. The highest and lowest scorers each defend their number using the criteria and specific moments in the excerpt
4. Room converges on a calibrated score; run a second excerpt and watch the spread shrink

**Facilitation Notes:**
- The gasp when a 1 and a 4 appear for the same call is the whole point — lean into it
- Ground it in the real thing: pull the excerpt from `get_call`, the criteria from `get_framework`, and use `competency_heatmap` to pick a competency your team actually disagrees on

**Debrief Questions:**
- "What drove the spread — the criteria, or our own assumptions?"
- "Which word in the criteria did we each interpret differently?"

**Link to Training:** If two coaches score the same call differently, reps get contradictory feedback. Calibration is how scoring becomes fair and trusted.

---

### Evidence, Not Opinion (Feedback Rewrite Drill)

**Purpose:** Convert vague, opinion-based feedback into criteria-first, transcript-evidence-backed coaching.

**Duration:** 15-20 minutes

**Setup:** A stack of woolly feedback lines ("felt a bit flat", "didn't really own the call", "needs more confidence"). Pairs, with a short call excerpt to hand. A bell for the vote.

**Exercise:**
1. Each pair draws a vague feedback line
2. In 3 minutes they rewrite it into feedback that names the competency, quotes or cites a specific moment from the excerpt, and states what "good" would have looked like
3. Pairs read the before and after; room rings the bell for any rewrite that still smuggles in opinion instead of evidence
4. Cleanest evidence-backed rewrite wins the round

**Facilitation Notes:**
- Ban comparative and vibe words ("more", "better", "confident") unless anchored to a criterion and a moment
- Use a real `get_call` excerpt so the evidence people cite is genuinely there to quote

**Debrief Questions:**
- "How did the rewrite change how the feedback would land with the rep?"
- "Which is harder — finding the evidence, or resisting the opinion?"

**Link to Training:** "Be more confident" is unactionable and easy to dispute. "At 4:12 you conceded the price the moment it was questioned — the criteria expect you to trade" is coaching a rep can actually use.

---

Every exercise should conclude with structured debrief:

1. **What happened?** (Observations, not judgments)
2. **What did you notice?** (Participant reflections)
3. **What does this mean for real calls?** (Application)
4. **What will you do differently?** (Commitment)

Keep debriefs tight. Better to run fewer exercises with good debriefs than many exercises with rushed endings.
